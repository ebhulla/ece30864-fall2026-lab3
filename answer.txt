a. What are some Pros and Cons of Agile development ( vs non-Agile)? List 2 of each.
Agile → Pros -  1. High Flexibility: Easily adapt to changing requirements and reprioritize the backlog.

 2. Fast Feedback: Deliver working features in a small chunks to get user input early.

              Cons - 1. Steep Learning Curve: Jira can feel complex trying to run a proper Agile Framework.

 2. Scope Creep: Constant Adjustments without strict limits can expand the project scope uncontrollably.

Non-Agile → Pros -  1. Clear Predictability: Fixed scopes make it easier to map out strict timelines upfront.

Simpler Setup: Basic linear tracking requires fewer advanced features, reducing initial tool clutter.

              Cons - 1. Rigid to Change: Altering core requirements mid projects is difficult and delays delivery.

Late Testing: Bugs and issues are usually discovered near the end of project cycle rather than continuously.


b. Explain the role and responsibilities of the Scrum Master vs the Product Owner.
Product Owner (PO): The PO represents the voice of the customer and the business. They decide what features gets built next.

Core Focus: Product value, return on investment (ROI), and stakeholder alignment.

Key Responsibilities: 1. Defines and communicates the Product Vision.

Creates, maintains and orders the Product Backlog (epics, user stories).

Scrum Master (SM) :  The SM is a servant leader who helps the team work efficiently. They ensure the team follows the Scrum rules and protects them from distractions.

Core Focus: Team Productivity, process health and continuous improvement.

Key responsibilities: 1. Facilitates Scrum events (Daily Standup, Sprint Planning).

   2. Removes impediments and blockers that slow the team down.


What are the 3 types of meetings that happen during a sprint cycle? List at least 2 points on what needs to be discussed during each of the meetings

1. Sprint Planning: This meeting kicks off the sprint to define what can be delivered and how to achieve it.

  a. The Sprint Goal: Discuss why the upcoming sprint is valuable and set a clear objective that aligns the team.

  b. Backlog Selection and Capacity: Review top priority product backlog items and evaluate team bandwidth/velocity to decided what works fits into the current cycle.

2. Daily Standup (Daily Scrum): A brief, recurring check-in held every day to synchronize the team.

  a. Progress Update: Each member shares what they completed since the last meeting and what they plan to work on today.

  b. Roadblocks and Blockers: Identify any obstacles or technical challenges slowing down the progress so the team can help resolve them.

3. Sprint Review: Help at the end of the sprint to showcase what was accomplished.

  a. Work Demonstration: The team presents the completed features, bug fixes or product increments to stakeholders.

  b. Feedback Gathering: Collect evaluation and feedback from the product owner and stakeholders to guide future backlog items.
